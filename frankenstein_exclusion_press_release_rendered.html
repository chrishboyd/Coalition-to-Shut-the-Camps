EVENT SNAPSHOT

Why it matters: The coalition is backing the AG's lawsuit while making clear that no single legal action is enough - every elected official with authority must use it, now

Detroit, MI - On Monday, May 18, members of the Coalition to Shut the Camps, alongside Indivisible: Downriver United 734, Detroit Will Breathe, No Detention Centers in Michigan, and partner organizations from across Southeast Michigan, will rally outside the Theodore Levin U.S. Courthouse in support of the State of Michigan and the City of Romulus' federal lawsuit against the U.S. Department of Homeland Security. The 9:30 AM rally precedes a 10:30 AM motion hearing in State of Michigan, et al. v. DHS (Case 2:26-cv-10968), the lawsuit filed by Attorney General Dana Nessel and the City of Romulus on March 24, 2026, challenging DHS's plan to convert a warehouse at 7525 Cogswell Street into an ICE detention facility.

The lawsuit raises seven counts under the National Environmental Policy Act, the Administrative Procedure Act, the Intergovernmental Cooperation Act, and the Immigration and Nationality Act. At its core, the complaint exposes a sequence DHS does not dispute: the federal government spent $34 million to purchase the warehouse on February 4, 2026, then waited seven weeks - until the same day Michigan filed suit - to produce any environmental analysis at all. NEPA, the ICA, and the INA required public notice, environmental review, and consultation with state and local officials BEFORE the purchase decision. DHS did none of it.

To paper over that record, DHS retroactively stacked three of its own internal 'categorical exclusions' - narrowly drawn exemptions written for low-impact routine actions like buying office supplies or replacing a roof - to claim that purchasing a warehouse, converting it into a mass detention facility, and operating it for the indefinite confinement of thousands of human beings required no environmental review whatsoever. Michigan's Attorney General calls this maneuver 'Frankenstein's Exclusion.' DHS's own sworn April 21 declaration confirms that two of the three stacked exemptions are 'non-documented' - meaning they produce no required written record of analysis at all - and that the Romulus site is engineered to scale to 2,000 detainees, four times the 500 figure DHS has repeated in public.

"Defendant impermissibly 'stacks' three CEs to create Frankenstein's Exclusion for the Romulus Warehouse."

- Michigan Attorney General Dana Nessel, Reply Brief, State of Michigan v. DHS, Case 2:26-cv-10968, April 28, 2026

On May 11, 2026, the ACLU of Michigan, the Michigan Immigrant Rights Center (MIRC), and the Detroit Justice Center filed to join the lawsuit - a significant escalation of the legal fight that brings deep civil rights, immigration law, and movement lawyering expertise to bear alongside the State and the City. The coalition welcomes their commitment. The breadth of legal firepower now aligned against this facility - the State Attorney General, the City of Romulus, and three of Michigan's most respected civil rights and immigrants' rights organizations - is itself a statement about how unacceptable, and how legally vulnerable, this project is.

That facility is publicly described as holding 500 people. DHS's own April 21 sworn declaration scales the number higher still - up to 2,000. The coalition has documented this discrepancy at every public action since February and is again demanding that elected officials and regulators stop treating ICE's stated numbers of 500 prisoners as credible.

The May 18 protest is part of the coalition's sustained, multi-front strategy targeting every regulatory, infrastructure, and political chokepoint that stands between this warehouse and an open detention facility. Solidarity Saturdays continue at the proposed site weekly. The coalition has pressed EGLE on permitting authority. It has pressed the Great Lakes Water Authority on infrastructure approvals. It has filed administrative requests, mobilized 30-plus partner organizations, and built sustained public pressure for more than three months. Now it is bringing that pressure to the courthouse, to the Governor's office, and to every facility in this state where Michiganders are being caged - including the GEO Group's North Lake Processing Center in Baldwin, where hundreds of people held by ICE recently undertook a multi-unit hunger strike to demand basic medical care, adequate food, and an end to their unjust captivity.

The coalition is calling on Governor Whitmer to direct EGLE and every other state agency under her authority to conduct a full, public, coordinated review of any activity that would advance the Romulus facility - and to publicly commit, as Pennsylvania, Maryland, and other states have done, that Michigan's infrastructure, permits, and public resources will not be used to scale federal immigration detention in this state. Governor Whitmer also holds appointment authority over the Great Lakes Water Authority Board and broader oversight responsibilities for the regional water authority - direct levers she has not used to ensure Michigan's public water system will not be conscripted into ICE detention infrastructure.

The coalition is similarly calling on the Wayne County Executive, the Wayne County Drain Commissioner, the Great Lakes Water Authority Board, and the Romulus City Council to publicly state - on the record, in writing - what authority they hold, what reviews are underway, and what conditions they will impose before any administrative action that would help this facility open. Every public regulatory refusal, every documented infrastructure concern, and every elected official on the record builds a wall of local resistance - and that wall does not just slow federal expansion. It strengthens the legal record the federal lawsuit depends on, and it makes Michigan a harder, costlier, and less politically tenable place for ICE to attempt warehouse detention.

The coalition's message to the federal government is equally direct: there is no path forward for an ICE warehouse concentration camp in Romulus, and there will never be a path forward for one anywhere in Michigan. Every water connection, every sewer approval, every utility commitment, every permit decision, every inspection, and every public silence that DHS is counting on to make this facility operational is being challenged by an organized, sustained, multi-front opposition. Communities in Maryland, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Virginia, and Georgia have already proven that warehouse detention can be stopped when officials choose to lead and communities refuse to stand down. Michigan will be on that list.

REPORTER CONTEXT & FRAMING ANGLES

-The motion hearing on May 18 is a substantive procedural moment in Case 2:26-cv-10968, the federal challenge filed by AG Dana Nessel and the City of Romulus against DHS, ICE, and their leadership on March 24, 2026. The lawsuit raises NEPA, APA, and Intergovernmental Cooperation Act claims - including DHS's failure to conduct environmental review at a floodplain site adjacent to a state-owned conservation easement.
-The lawsuit's core NEPA argument is that DHS retroactively invoked exemptions to evade the environmental review the law required before the purchase. DHS spent $34M acquiring the warehouse on February 4, 2026, and did not produce any environmental analysis until March 24, 2026 - the same day Michigan filed suit. The Michigan AG Reply Brief argues DHS stacked three internal categorical exclusions to manufacture a single composite exemption - a theory the brief labels 'Frankenstein's Exclusion.'
-DHS's own April 21 sworn declaration contains two material admissions reporters should examine. The property is engineered for '500 to 1,000 detainees, with scalability to accommodate 2,000,' and the central CATEX used to exempt the acquisition is 'a non-documented CATEX,' meaning DHS produced no written analysis to support the decision to buy the site.
-The Maryland precedent is directly applicable. The federal district court in Maryland v. Mullin held that a post hoc invocation of a categorical exclusion during litigation cannot justify a failure to prepare an EA or EIS.
-The coalition's framing is that the lawsuit is necessary, but not sufficient. Federal litigation against a hostile federal administration is a meaningful tool to buy time and force disclosures. It is not a substitute for the executive and regulatory authority held by the Governor, state agency directors, county officials, regional infrastructure boards, and municipal leadership.
-Local political action is litigation support. Every public regulatory refusal, every documented infrastructure concern, and every elected official on the record opposing this facility builds the factual and procedural record that strengthens the federal lawsuit.
-New legal entrants as of May 11. The ACLU of Michigan, the Michigan Immigrant Rights Center (MIRC), and the Detroit Justice Center filed to join the lawsuit on May 11, 2026.
-The Governor accountability frame is central. Coalition speakers will draw direct comparisons to Pennsylvania, Maryland, and Prince George's County, Maryland, while noting that Governor Whitmer has issued no equivalent directive in Michigan.
-The 500 vs. 1,600+ vs. 2,000 capacity discrepancy remains a core proof point. The complaint itself documents that the site's existing sewer infrastructure cannot support 500 detainees - let alone the 2,000 detainees DHS's own declaration reports as design capacity.
-The coalition's 'every lawful strategy' frame is multi-front by design. Litigation, regulation, infrastructure, local government, legislative action, executive action, and public mobilization are all active fronts.
-Federal context. The State and City's complaint alleges that the Eastern District of Michigan has addressed more than 120 habeas petitions contesting unlawful immigration detentions and has ruled that at least 89 detainees were unlawfully denied bond hearings.
-Coalition partners on site Monday will include representatives of the 30-plus coalition member organizations spanning faith, labor, environmental justice, immigrant rights, and grassroots democracy groups. Speakers will be available for interviews before and after the hearing.
